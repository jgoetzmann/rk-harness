# Equal thirds: explicit, adaptive and implicit get the same standing

Status as of 2026-09-10: **the rotation is armed and every batch has landed.**
`run.lane_schedule = 'EAI'`, so one cycle in three searches the explicit archive, one measures
the adaptive lane and one measures the implicit lane. Over its first 835 armed cycles the run
took 279 implicit, 278 explicit and 278 adaptive turns.

The order was the point. The split became data before anything read it, the two side classes got
open-ended work before they were given time, the site gave each class a page before there was
lane work to show, and every host viewer learned what a lane is before any lane existed. Arming
was the last step and the smallest one: a single config value.

This note is the plan. `docs/LANES.md` and `docs/LANESEARCH.md` describe the two machines it
builds; `docs/DECISIONS.md` D33 to D41 carry the rulings made along the way.

## 1. What the survey found, and why the refactor is not cosmetic

`docs/ROADMAP.md` has described a 70/15/15 split of attention since 2026-09-02. A survey on
2026-09-09 measured the preceding 186 cycles: 0.16 s of about 32,700 s reached adaptive and
implicit combined. That is 0.0005 percent against an intended 30.

Neither number was wrong when it was written. The intention lived in a sentence in a document
and the outcome lived nowhere at all, so nothing in the system was in a position to notice them
drifting apart. Three structural facts kept the gap open:

* Cycle 2726 spent 73 s on archive replay, 15 s on the directive, 64 s on four CMA-ES islands,
  18 s verifying and 6 s on the rest. The explicit search proper is 36 percent of a cycle;
  everything serving the explicit archive is 97.
* The two side tracks share one bounded 180 s slot every 20 cycles. That caps them at 4.9
  percent COMBINED even when the slot is full.
* The 103-point side-track catalogue has been complete since cycle 2540, so every firing since
  has logged `sidetrack_exhausted` in about 0.05 s. The slot was not merely small, it was empty.

The third fact is the one that orders the work. Giving the two lanes a third of the machine
before they have open-ended work would hand them a third of the clock and an empty queue.

## 2. The rule the batches follow

Every batch before B10 ships INERT or READ-ONLY, and says which in its own docstring. A batch
may add a module nothing calls, a document no page reads, or a viewer row that renders what
already exists. No batch before B10 changes what a cycle does.

Two invariants hold across all twelve, and each batch states them in its commit:

* `VERIFIER_HASH` stays `de5bec22...fd6e9`. Changing a pinned file is an epoch boundary, not a
  batch. The ten files are listed in CLAUDE.md rule 1.
* `sidetrack.code_hash()` stays `305c692a1b5c085a`. Moving it re-opens every measured
  side-track point, which is 103 points of work thrown away for an edit.

### The two axes, which B3 introduced and B6 cannot yet publish

Axis F is error at a fixed 65,536-cycle budget. That is what the scored archive measures and it
is the only axis an explicit fixed-step method has. Axis T is cycles-to-tolerance, and it is the
only axis on which an adaptive method can be compared at all: a method that chooses its own step
count cannot be scored at a step count chosen for it.

`rk-work/validation/axes.json` is where axis T lands. It is **not** on the traceability list
(the traceability list in CLAUDE.md), so no number from it reaches a page today. The class pages detect it, name
it, and say it has not been written. See section 5.

## 4. What B6 and B7 actually changed

B6 made `index.html` a hub: the three classes, the boundary between them, and one traced number
each with explicit first. The archive it used to carry moved verbatim to a new `explicit.html`.
`implicit.html` and `adaptive.html` are new and unconditional, so a fresh work directory still
writes all three; `sidetrack.html` keeps its filename and becomes provenance only. Nav is three
rows. Every `cell-*.html` filename and every inbound link is unchanged.

Absent data is the primary case, not the edge case, because a fresh work directory is what an
epoch starts from. With no ledger the class pages say so in a sentence and emit no card row at
all, rather than an empty table or a zero that reads as a measurement that came back empty.

B7 made the viewers lane-aware BEFORE the rotation is armed, which is the whole reason it
precedes B10. Every host viewer currently reads "cycle" as "explicit search": the stall counter,
the saturation orchestrator, `stats.txt` and the watcher all assume a cycle that appends nothing
to the scored archive has failed. Arming first would mean a release where a cycle that did
exactly what it was told reports a fault. `status.read_lanes` and `status.lane_rows` are the
single reader, so the watcher and `stats.txt` cannot disagree.

`saturation.py` is the viewer with teeth and B7 did not reach it; D35 finished it. Its 48-hour
no-progress rule counted wall clock, which was the same quantity as search time only because
every cycle was an explicit search. It now measures the window in explicit-lane seconds and
falls back to wall clock, saying which measure ran.

## 5. The decisions that were the owner's, and how they were settled

The owner delegated both on 2026-09-10. Both are recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

**`rk-work/validation/axes.json` stays off the traceability list (D39).** Axis T asks "how much
work to reach this accuracy". `benchmark/results.json` already answers that question across all
three classes on the same eight application problems, in its matched-accuracy table, and that
document is already on the list and already published. Admitting a second source for a claim the
site can already make lengthens a list whose whole value is its shortness. Two smaller facts point
the same way: the file does not exist yet, and `validation_axes.py` sits inside
`LANESEARCH_FILES`, so admitting it would move the lane digest for a document nobody has produced.
What would reopen it: a claim matched accuracy cannot support.

**The lane elites documents join the list; the raw lane records do not (D40).** Two thirds of the
machine's time now goes to the two lanes and none of its output was visible anywhere.
`rk-work/{adaptive,implicit}_archive/elites.json` is bounded at 32 entries, ranked by a stated
total order, schema-validated as `lane-elites/1`, and carries its own `statement` and `rule`
describing exactly what it ranked and over what. That is publishable with its framing attached.

The per-day records are not, and the distinction is the point. They are a search log at 18.3 KB
each, and a per-candidate number sitting on a page invites the one comparison that must never be
made: a lane record measures cycles-to-tolerance in float64 on the application problems, and a
scored archive record measures error at a fixed 65,536-cycle budget in Q15 on the seven frozen
problems. Those are opposite questions on different problems in different arithmetic.

Admission carries a framing requirement, the same one side-track numbers already carry: every
number from a lane elites document is rendered with its metric, its arithmetic, and the fact that
nothing in it is order-verified against the pinned checker.

**What it cost, paid deliberately.** `not_a_page_source` is written into every record and into
each elites document, so changing the publication contract changes what a record says.
`lanesearch.code_hash()` moves off `6a5be0d8f26639ea` and about 17,700 measured candidates become
"measured under earlier code". Nothing is deleted and nothing is wrong; the lanes re-enumerate.
That is the digest doing its job rather than waste: the two generations say different things about
themselves and should be distinguishable. At the throughput D41 sets, the adaptive lane re-covers
its 8,832 candidates in about 4 hours and the implicit lane its 8,864 in about 14.5.

**Still open, and deliberately not settled here:** D26's two questions on arming the exploration
policy, and P02's Q31 cost-model term. Q31 would move a pinned file, which is an epoch boundary
rather than a decision.

## 6. Arming: what it took, and how to undo it

The plan said not to arm until B8 had refilled the finite catalogue OR B9 had pointed the lanes
at `lanesearch`. The second happened, so B8 is superseded: `lanesearch` is an unbounded
enumeration per lane and cannot be exhausted, which is the property the catalogue lacked. B8
remains worth doing only if someone wants specific new parameter points measured by name.

The three runner changes the plan said must land WITH the arming did land with it, in B9:

1. `rk-work/schedule/cycles.jsonl` and `shares.json` are written every cycle, explicit included.
2. `cycle_done` carries a `lane` field, so `accept_rate` counts the explicit lane.
3. `stall_counter` advances only on an explicit lane.

`watchdog.no_candidate_minutes` was re-derived rather than changed: the longest run of
non-explicit letters in 'EAI' is two, so the worst gap between verified candidates is three
cycles, which is 8.8 minutes at the measured 176 s cadence and 15 at 300 s. 30 stands.

To revert: `python configure.py set run.lane_schedule=E`, then `.\stop.ps1` and `.\start.ps1`.
Nothing a lane wrote is in the scored archive, so reverting loses no scored work and invalidates
no score.

## 6b. What the run measured over its first 200 armed cycles

Read from `rk-work/schedule/shares.json`, the run's own record, over cycles 3607 to 3806.

| lane | cycles | share of seconds | its own work per cycle | per cycle total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| explicit | 66 | 47.6% | the whole cycle | 79.8 s |
| adaptive | 67 | 13.7% | 15.6 s | 22.6 s |
| implicit | 67 | 38.7% | 56.7 s | 63.8 s |

Exactly equal by cycle count, which is what the schedule defines and delivers. Not equal by
seconds, and the two suspected causes turned out to be one.

**The replay was not the problem.** The plan priced a full archive replay at 90 of every 182.9
seconds and made B12 the larger of the two gaps. Measured over 200 cycles the shared overhead is
about 7 seconds a cycle: the archive checkpoint is doing its work, and the whole cycle has come
down to 55 s on average from the 150 to 180 s seen in the opening hours. B12 is a small
optimisation, not a structural one.

**The candidate cap was the whole of it.** `lanesearch.MAX_CANDIDATES_PER_STEP` is 32, chosen when
a firing was a bounded side-track slot rather than a lane's whole turn. A candidate costs about
0.49 s adaptive and 1.77 s implicit, so 32 of them bound the adaptive lane at 15.6 s of a 180 s
budget and it handed back the other 164.

**What D41 changes.** The runner passes `max_candidates` from a new `run.lane_max_candidates`,
default 0 meaning no cap, so the time budget is the only thing that bounds a lane cycle. The
budget then becomes the equaliser, and it is set from the measurement rather than chosen:
`run.lane_max_seconds` goes 180 to 72, because an explicit cycle measured 79.8 s and a lane cycle
pays about 7 s of shared overhead. Each class now gets the same wall seconds per turn by
construction.

Expected per lane cycle at that budget: about 147 adaptive candidates and 41 implicit, against 32
for both before.

**The cost to watch is disk, and it is the one number here with a deadline.** A lane record is
18.3 KB, of which 81.6% is its `results` payload: 32 rows of per-problem, per-target measurement,
which is the evidence and not padding. At the new throughput the two archives grow at roughly
1.2 GB a day. `rk-work` sits on D:, which has 200 GB free, so the run has on the order of 160 days
before this needs an answer.

The answer, when it is needed, is a retention policy rather than a smaller budget: the ledger line
for a candidate is 392 bytes and the elites document is bounded, so identity and ranking are cheap
to keep forever. It is the raw per-day records that are large, and a lanesearch record is a pure
function of code, candidate and parameters, so a dropped one can be regenerated exactly. Nothing
here needs deciding until free space on D: falls under about 40 GB.

## 7. What is left

**Nothing that blocks anything.** All twelve batches have landed or been superseded, the
rotation is armed, both traceability questions are settled, and the lane budget is set from
measurement rather than from a guess.

**B12, the replay cache, is a smaller prize than the plan thought.** It was written up when a
cycle cost 150 to 180 s and the replay looked like 90 of them. Measured over 200 armed cycles the
shared overhead is about 7 s a cycle: the archive checkpoint landed and the whole cycle came down
to 55 s. Caching the replay on lane cycles would buy roughly 7 s in 80, which is worth having and
is not worth a restart on its own.

**The one number with a deadline is disk**, and section 6b carries it: about 1.2 GB a day into
the two lane archives, roughly 160 days of headroom on D:, and a retention policy rather than a
smaller budget as the answer when it is needed. Nothing to decide until free space falls under
about 40 GB.

**Two rulings from other work remain open and are not part of this refactor:** D26's two
questions on arming the exploration policy, and P02's Q31 cost-model term. Q31 would move a
pinned file, which is an epoch boundary rather than a decision.

**One stale recital, left deliberately.** `rk_harness/validation_axes.py` and
`rk_harness/lanes.py` each carry a sentence reciting the traceability list. Both were brought
current in the same digest move that landed D40. If the list changes again, `validation_axes.py`
sits inside `LANESEARCH_FILES`, so editing it re-opens every lane candidate: fold that edit into
the next move that has to happen anyway rather than paying for it alone.
